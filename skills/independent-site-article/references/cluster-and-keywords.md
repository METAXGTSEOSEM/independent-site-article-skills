# Topic clusters, keywords and cannibalization

Plan the article as one page in a cluster, not as an isolated keyword target.
The cluster map and keyword plan come before the outline.

## Page roles

| Role | Job | Typical URL | Internal-link direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pillar / hub | Explain the category and route readers to sub-topics | Category or guide hub | Links to all cluster pages |
| Cluster article | Solve one intent or question | Blog or resource page | Links up to the pillar and sideways to relevant pages |
| Product / category page | Support selection or purchase | Product or category URL | Receives links from selection, comparison and application articles |
| Comparison page | Help a choice between options | Comparison URL | Links to the relevant product or category pages |
| Case study | Show a permitted real outcome | Case-study URL | Links to the relevant application or product page |
| FAQ / glossary | Define a term or answer a narrow question | FAQ or glossary URL | Links to the relevant pillar or cluster page |

If the site already has these pages, use the existing structure. Do not create a
new URL merely because a keyword is slightly different.

## Cannibalization check

Before assigning a focus keyword, check existing URLs, titles, H1s, meta
descriptions, sitemaps and internal links where available.

Potential cannibalization exists when two or more pages target the same:

- market and language;
- primary intent;
- focus keyword or close variant;
- reader decision;
- page role and CTA.

Choose one action and record it:

- merge the pages;
- differentiate the intent or page role;
- make one page the canonical target;
- link the weaker page to the stronger page;
- noindex or retire a thin page;
- keep both only with evidence that the intents are genuinely different.

Do not solve cannibalization by adding more keywords to both pages.

## Keyword sources

Ask the owner first:

> Do you already have a keyword list with search volume, intent and target URLs?

If yes, use it and preserve the source and date. Check that the intent still
matches the page.

If no, offer two explicit paths:

1. Use DataForSEO or another authorized data source when available.
2. Use AI-generated keyword hypotheses only as unverified candidates, clearly
   labeled; never present them as measured volume, difficulty or ranking data.

Do not invent search volume, difficulty, CPC, traffic or competitor data. Record
the tool, market, language, date and access limits.

Before assigning a focus keyword, require a clear intent and either measured
volume from an authorized source or an explicit owner decision to proceed
without volume. If volume is unavailable, mark the keyword plan as unvalidated
and do not describe the keyword as proven demand.

## Keyword plan

Use `assets/keyword-plan.csv`. Every row needs:

- keyword and role: focus / secondary / supporting entity;
- intent: informational / commercial / transactional / navigational;
- market and language;
- volume and difficulty only when measured;
- source and checked date;
- target URL and page role;
- cannibalization status;
- notes and approval.

One URL gets one focus keyword. Secondary keywords support the same intent; they
are not a list of unrelated queries.

## Mapping without density rules

- Focus keyword: title, H1, URL and first meaningful paragraph when natural.
- Secondary keywords: H2s and body where they clarify the reader's question.
- Supporting entities: names, standards, materials, roles and related concepts
  that make the page complete.
- Do not force a keyword into every paragraph.
- Do not use keyword density, exact-match counts or repeated phrases as a
  quality target.
- The title, H1, URL and meta description must be unique across the cluster.

## Internal links

Plan links before drafting:

- cluster page to pillar;
- pillar to cluster pages;
- selection/comparison/application articles to product or category pages;
- case studies to the relevant application and product pages;
- product pages to useful guides where the reader needs education.

Use descriptive anchors that describe the destination. Verify every target is
live and not a draft. Record links in the cluster map or SEO/GEO delivery sheet.

## Batch diversity

When producing more than one article:

- compare titles, meta descriptions, openings, H2 sequences and CTAs;
- do not reuse the same "what is / why it matters / how to choose / conclusion"
  skeleton for every type;
- vary examples and evidence, not just wording;
- check that two articles do not target the same intent and focus keyword;
- record the differentiator for each page.

A batch can share standards and evidence, but not an identical template
fingerprint.
