# Content types and article frameworks

Choose one primary content type before research or outlining. The type defines
the reader's job, required sections, evidence priorities, CTA and review gates.
It does not set a fixed word count, keyword density, paragraph length or a
promise of rankings.

If the brief names a type, use it. If it does not, infer from the reader's query
and decision stage and state the assumption. If two types compete, choose the
one the page must support first; treat the other as a link or a follow-up.

## The eight types

| Type | Reader question | Typical titles | Required sections | Evidence priorities | CTA | Main failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Awareness / education** | What is it? | What is X; X explained; types of X | Definition, why it matters, categories, how it works, who it fits, next step | Definitions, official explainers, standards, expert sources | Learn more or choose a next guide | Premature sales pitch or an unverified definition |
| **Selection / buying guide** | How do I choose? | How to choose X; X buying guide | Reader task, criteria, trade-offs, shortlist method, evidence checklist, next step | Current specifications, tests, prices and approved claims | Quote, product page or comparison | A universal "best" without criteria or evidence |
| **Comparison / alternative** | X or Y? | X vs Y; X alternatives; which is better | Like-for-like basis, differences table, fit by scenario, limitations, decision rule | Current versions, matching configurations, independent evidence | Comparison sheet, quote or product page | Comparing different configurations or inventing a winner |
| **Specification / standard** | What must it meet? | Specifications; standards; technical requirements | Scope, units, applicable standard, test method, compatibility, verification | Current standards, manufacturer datasheets, jurisdiction | Datasheet, technical consult or RFQ | Mixing units, versions or jurisdictions |
| **Application / scenario** | Is it right for my situation? | Best for hotels; for warehouses; for retailers | Scenario, constraints, configuration, evidence or case, implementation, limits | Real project evidence and permission, site conditions | Quote, consultation or case study | Invented cases or ignoring site constraints |
| **Troubleshooting / process** | How do I diagnose or solve it? | Troubleshooting; why X happens; manufacturing process | Symptom, causes, diagnostic steps, safety limits, when to escalate | Technical manuals, repair data, process evidence, safety guidance | Support, service or expert review | Guessing a cause or omitting a safety escalation |
| **Certification / compliance** | What does compliance require? | Certification explained; compliance requirements | Scope, jurisdiction, process, documents, responsibilities, limits | Regulator, standards body, notified body or official guidance | Compliance documents or expert consult | Presenting general information as legal advice |
| **Procurement / cooperation** | How do I buy or qualify a supplier? | OEM vs ODM; MOQ; lead time; supplier audit | Cooperation model, qualification criteria, information request, commercial terms, next step | Actual company terms, approved capability claims, audit evidence | RFQ, sample request or contact | Inventing MOQ, lead time, capacity or certification |

The labels are B2B-oriented, but the same jobs apply to B2C content. A B2C
selection guide may use price, delivery and returns as criteria; a B2B guide may
use specifications, service and qualification.

## Type is not the whole plan

The same type still changes with the reader and the cluster role:

- A selection guide for a procurement lead leads with comparison, terms and
  qualification; for an owner it leads with commercial risk and responsibility;
  for an engineer it leads with constraints and verification.
- A cluster article solves one intent and links up to its pillar; a pillar page
  explains the category and routes readers to multiple cluster pages.
- A comparison page and a product page may both support a purchase, but they
  must not target the same intent and focus keyword.

Read `audience-and-voice.md` and `cluster-and-keywords.md` before turning the type
into an outline.

## How to select the type

1. What must the reader decide or do after reading?
2. Where are they in the journey: understand, choose, compare, specify, apply,
   diagnose, comply or buy?
3. What evidence can actually be supplied? Missing central evidence limits the
   type or blocks the article.
4. What action follows? The CTA must match the type and business model.

Do not force one article to be a definition, comparison, buying guide and sales
page at once. If the reader needs all four, split the work or define one primary
type with clearly linked follow-ups.

## Type-specific review gates

| Type | Gate before `PASS` |
| --- | --- |
| Awareness | The definition and categories are verified; the page does not sell before it explains. |
| Selection | Every criterion is traceable; no universal "best"; the shortlist method is usable. |
| Comparison | Versions and configurations are comparable; limitations and fit are explicit. |
| Specification | Units, versions, test methods and jurisdiction are stated; unresolved values are marked. |
| Application | Cases and constraints are real and permitted; the recommendation fits the scenario. |
| Troubleshooting | Diagnosis is evidence-based; safety and escalation limits are visible. |
| Certification | The official source and scope are current; the page does not masquerade as legal advice. |
| Procurement | Commercial terms are approved and current; no MOQ, lead time or capacity is invented. |

## Guardrails

- Do not invent first-person factory experience, site visits, test data,
  certifications, customer stories or expert credentials.
- Do not use a fixed word count as a quality target.
- Do not present keyword density or keyword placement as a ranking rule.
- Do not copy a competitor outline without checking whether its sections fit the
  reader and the available evidence.
- Do not assign two URLs the same intent and focus keyword.
- Record the anti-template difference from sibling pages.
- Do not turn one article into a complete site-wide topic-cluster plan.
- Mark missing type-critical evidence as a blocker instead of writing around it.
